# Code of Conduct

**Project:** Open Digital Continuity Standard (ODCS)  
**Document ID:** ODCS-CODE-001  
**Version:** 0.1.0-draft  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Applies to:** All project spaces and community activities

---

## 1. Purpose

The Open Digital Continuity Standard (ODCS) project is committed to building an open, technically rigorous, vendor-neutral specification for reliable digital experiences when network connectivity is intermittent or unavailable.

This Code of Conduct establishes expectations for everyone participating in the project. It aims to create an environment where people can collaborate respectfully, challenge technical ideas constructively, and contribute regardless of their background or level of experience.

Participation in ODCS project spaces constitutes agreement to follow this policy.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all participants in ODCS activities, including:

- GitHub repositories, issues, pull requests, and discussions.
- Technical specification reviews and design proposals.
- Documentation, examples, and implementation discussions.
- Online meetings, community events, and project-related communications.
- Public communications when an individual is officially representing ODCS.

Project maintainers may clarify how this policy applies to particular situations.

## 3. Expected Behavior

All participants are expected to:

### 3.1 Be respectful

- Treat others with dignity and professionalism.
- Communicate disagreements without personal attacks.
- Recognize that contributors have different levels of experience.
- Give constructive feedback focused on the work rather than the individual.
- Respect reasonable boundaries and requests to discontinue unwanted interactions.

### 3.2 Collaborate constructively

- Explain technical opinions using evidence, reasoning, and relevant references.
- Welcome questions, corrections, and alternative approaches.
- Acknowledge useful contributions from others.
- Accept reasonable criticism and revise proposals when warranted.
- Separate technical disagreement from personal conflict.

### 3.3 Maintain technical integrity

- Do not knowingly misrepresent the maturity, adoption, capabilities, or conformance status of ODCS.
- Distinguish proposed requirements from approved requirements.
- Identify assumptions, limitations, and unresolved questions.
- Cite external standards, research, and prior art where relevant.
- Report errors, security concerns, and misleading documentation responsibly.
- Do not claim that ODCS is certified, officially recognized, or universally adopted without verifiable authorization and evidence.

### 3.4 Respect intellectual property

- Contribute material that you have the right to submit.
- Preserve applicable copyright notices and license information.
- Clearly identify third-party material and its licensing conditions.
- Do not present another person's work as your own.
- Follow the project's contribution and licensing requirements.

### 3.5 Protect privacy and security

- Do not publish another person's private information without permission.
- Avoid posting credentials, access tokens, private keys, or confidential data.
- Report security vulnerabilities through an appropriate private channel when public disclosure could create risk.
- Respect applicable privacy, security, and confidentiality obligations.

## 4. Unacceptable Behavior

The following conduct is prohibited in ODCS project spaces:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, or deliberate humiliation.
- Discriminatory language or conduct targeting personal characteristics.
- Personal attacks, insults, or sustained hostile behavior.
- Sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing private information without consent.
- Deliberate disruption of discussions, reviews, or project activities.
- Repeated unwanted contact after a clear request to stop.
- Impersonation, fraudulent attribution, or deliberate misrepresentation of project decisions.
- Knowingly submitting malicious content, sabotaging project resources, or attempting unauthorized access.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith.
- Other conduct that a reasonable and consistently applied interpretation of this policy determines to be inappropriate.

Good-faith technical criticism, respectful disagreement, and evidence-based challenges to project decisions are not violations of this policy merely because they are uncomfortable or unpopular.

## 5. Technical Disagreement and Review

ODCS encourages rigorous review and debate.

Participants may challenge proposed requirements, question design choices, identify weaknesses, and recommend competing solutions.

Technical objections should, where practical:

1. Identify the requirement or proposal being challenged.
2. Explain the technical concern.
3. Provide supporting evidence, examples, or references.
4. Describe potential alternatives and trade-offs.
5. Allow other participants a reasonable opportunity to respond.

No participant is required to agree with a proposal simply to maintain harmony. Decisions should be based on documented reasoning and the project's published governance process.

## 6. Reporting a Concern

Anyone who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this policy is encouraged to report it.

A report should include, where possible:

- A description of the incident.
- The relevant date and project location.
- Links, screenshots, or other supporting evidence.
- The names or usernames of the people involved, if known.
- Any immediate safety or confidentiality concerns.

**Reporting channel:** The project maintainers must publish a verified private reporting method before this document is treated as having an operational confidential reporting process.

Until such a method is established, concerns may be raised through the repository's available contact mechanisms, with sensitive details kept out of public issues whenever possible.

Do not include passwords, authentication tokens, private keys, or unrelated personal information in a report.

Reports will be handled as discreetly as reasonably possible. Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where disclosure is necessary to investigate or respond to a concern.

## 7. Review and Investigation

When a report is received, the responsible maintainers should:

1. Acknowledge receipt when reasonably possible.
2. Review the available information fairly and impartially.
3. Give relevant parties an appropriate opportunity to provide context.
4. Protect sensitive information to the extent reasonably possible.
5. Document the decision and rationale proportionately to the seriousness of the matter.
6. Communicate the outcome to the relevant parties when appropriate.

Maintainers should avoid investigating complaints in which they have a material personal conflict of interest. Where feasible, another uninvolved maintainer or mutually acceptable reviewer should handle the matter.

The project must not promise a particular investigation timeline until a practical response process has been established.

## 8. Enforcement

Depending on the circumstances, the project may take one or more of the following actions:

1. **Informal guidance:** Explain the concern and request corrective behavior.
2. **Warning:** Provide a clear statement identifying the conduct that must stop.
3. **Temporary restrictions:** Limit participation in a particular discussion or project space.
4. **Temporary suspension:** Suspend project participation for a defined period.
5. **Removal:** Remove a participant from project spaces or revoke project privileges where justified.

Serious misconduct may warrant stronger action without prior informal guidance.

Enforcement decisions should consider the severity, context, frequency, impact, and available evidence. Measures should be proportionate, consistent, and documented appropriately.

Where practical, participants may request a review of an enforcement decision by an uninvolved maintainer. The project should publish a formal appeal process as its governance matures.

## 9. Responsibilities of Maintainers

Project maintainers are responsible for applying this policy fairly within the spaces they administer.

They should:

- Model respectful and constructive behavior.
- Explain project rules and review expectations.
- Respond appropriately to reported concerns.
- Avoid using enforcement powers to silence legitimate technical criticism.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest.
- Protect sensitive information where reasonably possible.
- Document significant enforcement decisions.
- Apply this policy consistently, regardless of a participant's role or seniority.

Maintainer status does not exempt anyone from this Code of Conduct.

## 10. No Retaliation

Retaliation against a person who reports a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, or requests a policy review is prohibited.

Knowingly submitting a malicious or deliberately fabricated report may itself be reviewed under this policy. A report made in good faith will not be treated as misconduct merely because it cannot be substantiated.

## 11. Relationship to Other Project Policies

This Code of Conduct complements the project's other documents, including:

- `CONTRIBUTING.md` — contribution procedures and review expectations.
- `GOVERNANCE.md` — project authority, decision-making, and accountability, once established.
- `LICENSE` — applicable software and documentation licensing terms.
- `SECURITY.md` — vulnerability reporting and security handling, once established.

If these documents appear to conflict, maintainers should clarify the applicable policy transparently and update the documentation to resolve the ambiguity.

This Code of Conduct does not replace applicable laws or create legal rights beyond those otherwise available.

## 12. Administration and Changes

This document is a proposed community policy for ODCS version 0.1.0-draft.

Until a formal governance structure is established, the repository's current maintainers are responsible for administering the policy within the project spaces they control.

Changes should be proposed through a documented pull request or another publicly reviewable project process, except where confidential handling is necessary for safety or privacy.

Material changes should include a rationale and an effective date.

## 13. Acknowledgment

By participating in ODCS project spaces, contributors are expected to follow this Code of Conduct and help maintain a respectful, inclusive, and technically rigorous community.

**The objective is simple: challenge ideas rigorously, treat people respectfully, and build trustworthy standards together.**
